Visual Inertial Odometry

Enabling precise localization for Micro Aerial Vehicles in GPS-denied environments.

Institution

Nanyang Technological University

Role

Research Associate

Duration

Aug 2018 – April 2019

Location

Singapore

Introduction

Micro Aerial Vehicles (MAVs) are increasingly used for indoor inspection, search and rescue, and autonomous delivery. However, GPS signals are unavailable indoors, making alternative localization methods essential.

At NTU's Robotics Research Centre, I worked on deploying Visual Inertial Odometry (VIO) and SLAM algorithms on resource-constrained embedded systems, enabling MAVs to navigate autonomously in challenging indoor and outdoor environments.

The Challenge

Deploying perception algorithms on MAVs presents unique constraints:

"Every gram matters on a drone. We had limited compute, limited power, and milliseconds to make decisions."

  • Real-time performance on Nvidia Jetson TX2
  • Rapid motion causing image blur
  • Varying lighting conditions (indoor/outdoor transitions)
  • Vibrations from propellers affecting IMU data
  • Limited payload capacity for sensors

The Solution

I implemented and optimized state-of-the-art VIO algorithms including VINS-Mono and VINS-Fusion for the Jetson TX2 platform. Key contributions included:

  • Real-time feature tracking optimized for GPU acceleration
  • Tightly-coupled visual-inertial fusion for robust state estimation
  • Loop closure using DBoW2 vocabulary for global consistency
  • Multi-sensor integration with stereo cameras and Lidar

Tech Stack

C++ ROS CUDA OpenCV Ceres Solver Jetson TX2

Impact

The optimized VIO system enabled stable autonomous flight in GPS-denied environments, contributing to NTU's research in aerial robotics.
