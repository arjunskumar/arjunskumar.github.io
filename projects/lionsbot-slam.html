3D Lidar SLAM

Enabling drift-free localization for autonomous cleaning robots in large-scale indoor environments.

Company

Lionsbot International

Role

Lead Autonomy Engineer

Duration

Feb 2022 – Present

Location

Singapore

Introduction

Autonomous cleaning robots need to navigate complex indoor environments like malls, airports, and warehouses — often spanning 50,000+ square meters with dynamic obstacles and changing layouts.

Traditional 2D Lidar SLAM struggled in these environments due to drift accumulation over large distances and inability to handle multi-floor scenarios. My mission was to develop a robust 3D SLAM system that could maintain centimeter-level accuracy across entire facilities.

The Challenge

Large-scale environments present unique challenges for localization systems:

"When your robot operates for 8+ hours across 50,000 sqm, even small drift errors compound into meters of localization error."

Key technical challenges included:

  • Drift accumulation in featureless corridors
  • Dynamic obstacles (people, carts, temporary structures)
  • Glass walls and reflective surfaces confusing lidar
  • Real-time performance on embedded hardware
  • Seamless map updates without downtime

The Solution

I designed and implemented a multi-sensor fusion SLAM system combining 3D Lidar, wheel odometry, and IMU data through a factor graph optimization framework.

Key innovations included:

  • Loop closure detection using scan context descriptors
  • Robust outlier rejection for dynamic obstacle handling
  • Incremental map updates without full re-mapping
  • Multi-session SLAM for persistent localization across power cycles

Tech Stack

C++17 ROS 2 GTSAM PCL Eigen Docker

Impact

The new SLAM system is now deployed across Lionsbot's fleet of autonomous cleaning robots operating in commercial facilities across Asia and Europe.
